[p1] UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT SOUTHERN DISTRICT OF NEW YORK. SHENZHEN YINGFAN E-COMMERCE CO., LTD., Petitioner, -v- AMAZON.COM SERVICES LLC and AMAZON.COM INC., Respondents. LEWIS J. LIMAN, United States District Judge: Petitioner Shenzhen Yingfan E-Commerce Co., Ltd. (“Petitioner”) applies for an Order partially confirming and partially modifying a final arbitration award issued by the Honorable Lynne Bernabei on June 5, 2025. Dkt. No. 8. Respondents Amazon.com Services LLC (“Amazon LLC”) and Amazon.com, Inc. (“Amazon Inc.” and with Amazon LLC, “Amazon” or “Respondents”) oppose the motion. Dkt. Nos. 13–14. For the following reasons, the Motion to Partially Confirm and Partially Modify the Arbitration Award is denied. Petitioner is a foreign corporation with its principal place of business in Shenzhen, China. Id. ¶ 1. It also is a third-party seller on Amazon.com.

[p2] As a third-party seller on Amazon.com, Petitioner was required to sign Respondents’ Business Solutions Agreement (“BSA”), which establishes terms and conditions for selling on the website. The BSA also contains a binding arbitration provision. It provides that, with limited exceptions not applicable here, the parties consent that “any dispute with Amazon or its Affiliates or claim relating in any way to [the BSA] or your use of the Services will be resolved by binding arbitration” before the American Arbitration Association under its commercial rules. Id. § 18.

[p3] However, according to Petitioner, in or around December 7, 2021, without prior notice and any substantiating evidence for alleged breaches, Amazon abruptly deactivated Petitioner’s seller accounts on the basis that Petitioner had engaged in “review abuse.” At the same time, Amazon also seized all of Petitioner’s sales proceeds then held by it—a sum of $266,219.33. On November 22, 2023, Petitioner filed a request for arbitration, seeking to recover its sales proceeds and other damages and declaratory relief. After the parties engaged in discovery and submitted extensive briefing, the arbitrator issued a Partial Final Award on March 25, 2025.

[p4] On April 30, 2025, the arbitrator issued an “Order Noticing Hearing on Damages.” Dkt. No. 14-7. The order recounted that the parties and the arbitrator had held a prehearing conference that day and scheduled a hearing on damages for May 15, 2025 “in order to accommodate the schedules of counsel in China.” Id. It directed that briefs setting forth the parties’ respective positions with respect to damages were due on May 9, 2025. Id.

[p5] One week before the scheduled date for the hearing, on the evening of May 7, 2025, Petitioner’s counsel requested that, “[d]ue to a scheduling conflict with another hearing,” the hearing be rescheduled to 10:00 a.m. EST on May 16 or May 19, 2025. Dkt. No. 14-8. The arbitrator responded that she was not available at either of the times proposed by Petitioner, directed the parties to confer about other dates from 5:00 to 8:00 p.m. EST on May 19 or May 20, 2025 during which a hearing could be held, invited the parties to consider submitting affidavits from their business officers attesting to damages in lieu of a live hearing, and directed the parties to respond as to a suitable date and time by no later than close of business on May 12, 2025. Id. On May 9, 2025, the parties submitted their respective briefs on damages. Dkt. No. 14-11. Petitioner, however, did not meet the May 12 deadline to submit a suitable date or time for the damages hearing, nor did Amazon. On May 13, 2025, the arbitrator informed the parties that she was happy to rule on the basis of the filings “and if either or both parties want to have a hearing we could do it at 7:00 p.m. on May 19.” Dkt. No. 14-8 at 7. Later that day, Petitioner’s counsel informed the arbitrator that Petitioner was agreeable to a ruling “based solely on the filings.” Id. at 6. Amazon responded on May 16 that its witness was available for a hearing on May 20, 2025, at 7:00 p.m. EST. Id. at 5.

[p6] That same day the arbitrator then scheduled the hearing for 7:00 p.m. EST on May 20. Id. at 4. On May 19, 2026, the day before the hearing, Petitioner responded that it was unavailable for a hearing on May 20 and urged that the arbitrator not conduct a live hearing at all. Petitioner wrote that counsel “is unavailable for the hearing scheduled on May 20 at 7:00 PM EST due to a scheduling conflict with another hearing” and that “[t]his approach aligns with the explicit terms of Section 18 of the Business Solutions Agreement (BSA), which grants the Seller the choice of arbitration method. Claimant chose the resolution based on written submissions.” Id. at 3. Petitioner wrote again on the morning of May 20 that it could not “attend the call today due to a scheduling conflict.” Dkt. No. 14-8 at 2. The arbitrator responded: “Due to the late notification of non-availability, we are having the hearing as scheduled. The respondent is not available other days this week, and I am not available next week. If you wish to make additional submissions prior to the hearing, I will be happy to review them.” Id. at 1–2.

[p7] That evening, the arbitrator sent a message to all parties indicating that the hearing on damages had been held and that she had considered the pleadings filed as well as the testimony presented by Amazon and inviting Petitioner to make additional submissions by midnight that day. Dkt. No. 14-10. Petitioner did not do so. Dkt. No. 15 at 10.

[p8] On June 5, 2025, the arbitrator issued her Final Award, which granted Petitioner the return of $199,805.64 in withheld funds and $49,038.75 in consequential damages. Dkt. No. 14-9 at 2. The arbitrator rejected Petitioner’s proposed damages figure and adopted in full the damages proposed by Amazon. Id. at 2.

[p10] On July 28, 2025, the arbitrator denied the motion to modify, reaffirming the existing measure of damages. The arbitrator wrote: “Third, I deny Claimant’s Application with respect to the scheduling of the arbitration. I scheduled the arbitration hearing three times. … Therefore, I held the arbitration hearing on May 20, 2025, and allowed Claimant’s counsel to file additional pleadings if she felt it necessary. She did not show up for the hearing on May 20, 2025 or file additional pleadings. Therefore, I closed the record and issued an award.” Dkt. No. 10-16.

[p11] On October 29, 2025, Petitioner filed a Petition to Confirm and Modify Arbitration Award with the Supreme Court of the State of New York, County of New York. Dkt. No. 1-1. On January 2, 2026, Respondents timely filed a Notice of Removal to this Court. Dkt. No. 1. Petitioner asks the Court to confirm the award in part and to vacate it in part. It asks the Court to confirm the award as to liability but to vacate or modify the award with respect to damages pursuant to Federal Arbitration Act (“FAA”), 9 U.S.C. §§ 10, 11, for arbitral misconduct, manifest disregard for the law, and miscalculation. Dkt. No. 10 at 2. Respondents do not seek vacatur with respect to the award but argue that the Court should deny Petitioner’s motion for confirmation for lack of standing. Respondents oppose the motion to vacate or modify the award with respect to damages. Dkt. No. 14 at 1. Petitioner seeks partial confirmation of the Award to the extent that it finds Sections 2, 3, and 8 of the BSA unenforceable. Respondents contend that it has satisfied the Award by paying $248,844.39, the amount specified by the Final Award, to Petitioner. On August 22, 2025, Petitioner acknowledged receiving Respondents’ payment. Dkt. No. 14-13 at 1.

[p12] Finally, “Article III’s case-or-controversy requirement applies to actions governed by the FAA.” Stafford v. Int’l Bus. Machs. Corp., 78 F.4th 62, 68 (2d Cir. 2023); Badgerow v. Walters, 596 U.S. 1, 4 (2022). An order confirming an arbitration award is different than an order vacating it and provides different relief. See Berkowitz v. Gould Paper Corp., 2022 WL 118232, at *3 (S.D.N.Y. Jan. 12, 2022) (noting that “court must grant motion to confirm unless the award is vacated, modified, or corrected” under the FAA (quoting 9 U.S.C. § 9)). That Petitioner may have standing to seek vacatur thus does not demonstrate that it has standing to seek confirmation.

[p13] Stafford is on all fours. It is not disputed that Respondents have satisfied the award. Thus, Petitioner has already obtained through Petitioner’s compliance everything it would be entitled to obtain through a judgment. Petitioner lacks standing to bring suit seeking confirmation of the Award. Petitioner also asks the Court to vacate or modify the award with respect to damages. Petitioner argues that the arbitrator committed “misconduct” under Section 10(a) of the FAA by refusing to grant its request for a postponement of the May 20, 2025 hearing and by failing to consider “pertinent and material” evidence prior to reaching her judgment on damages.

[p14] Section 10(a) of the FAA permits a court to vacate an arbitral award on limited grounds. The statute permits an award to be vacated only under one of four circumstances, including “(3) where the arbitrators were guilty of misconduct in refusing to postpone the hearing, upon sufficient cause shown, or in refusing to hear evidence pertinent and material to the controversy; or of any other misbehavior by which the rights of any party have been prejudiced.” 9 U.S.C. § 10(a). Section (11)(a) of the FAA permits the court to modify an award if “there was an evident material miscalculation of figures or an evident material mistake in the description of any person, thing, or property referred to in the award.” 9 U.S.C. § 11(a).

[p16] Petitioner argues that the arbitrator committed misconduct under Section 10(a)(3) of the FAA by declining to postpone the May 20, 2025 hearing on damages. Section 10(a)(3) of the FAA permits the vacatur of an arbitral award “where the arbitrators were guilty of misconduct in refusing to postpone the hearing, upon sufficient cause shown, or in refusing to hear evidence pertinent and material to the controversy; or of any other misbehavior by which the rights of any party have been prejudiced.” 9 U.S.C. § 10(a)(3).

[p22] Petitioner has not shown that the arbitrator here engaged in misconduct—either through the unreasonable refusal to postpone the hearing or the failure to review pertinent evidence—sufficient to support vacatur of any portion of the award. The arbitrator had a reasonable basis for not further adjourning the hearing. The arbitrator had a more than “colorable” basis for denying Petitioner’s request for an adjournment. Moreover, there was nothing fundamentally unfair in the arbitrator’s method of proceeding and Petitioner has demonstrated no prejudice. And the arbitrator offered Petitioner the opportunity to make additional submissions both prior to the hearing, Dkt. No. 14-8 at 1–2, and after the hearing, Dkt. No. 14-10. Petitioner eschewed both opportunities.

[p30] At their core, these are substantive claims that the arbitrator erred in her interpretation of the BSA, not a claim of mathematical mistake.

[p33] CONCLUSION. Petitioner’s motion to confirm the arbitral award as to liability is DENIED for lack of standing. Petitioner’s motion to modify or vacate the award is DENIED. The Clerk of Court is respectfully directed to close Dkt. No. 10 and to close this case. Dated: July 21, 2026 New York, New York. LEWIS J. LIMAN, United States District Judge.
